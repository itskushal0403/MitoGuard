How it works

Detectors propose, a pathology foundation model decides, and conformal prediction quantifies trust.

Stage 1

Imposter-aware candidates

Two YOLO11 detectors with explicit mitosis and imposter classes, trained with and without stain augmentation. They keep both class scores, because on new scanners real mitoses often get filed as "imposter".

Stage 2 · novelty

Cell-centred foundation model

A mitosis covers ~50 of 224 px, so the usual CLS embedding mostly describes the surrounding tissue. Zooming in and reading out the centre patch tokens lifts validation AP from 0.77 to 0.90. Phikon-v2, Phikon and Lunit are then LoRA-fine-tuned.

Decision

Robust to scanner shift

The final call ignores YOLO's confidence, which is the signal that breaks on unseen scanners. An ensemble with 8-view test-time augmentation decides, with Platt-calibrated probabilities (ECE 0.017).

Trust

Intervals + ambiguity flag

Conformal prediction turns the Poisson-binomial count into a 90% interval. If the interval crosses a grade cut-off (≤7 / 8–14 / ≥15), the region is flagged for review.

Results on domains the model never saw

Leave-one-tumour-out (7 folds) and leave-one-scanner-out (4 folds) on MIDOG++ (503 regions, 11,937 mitoses, 14,349 imposters). Nested model selection: a held-out domain never influences its own configuration. 95% CIs from a paired bootstrap.
